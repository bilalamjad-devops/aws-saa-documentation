Category: CSAA – Design Resilient Architectures

A company installed sensors to track the number of people who visit the park. The data is sent every day to an Amazon Kinesis stream with default settings for processing, in which a consumer is configured to process the data every other day. The employee noticed that the Amazon S3 bucket is not receiving all of the data that is being sent to the Kinesis stream. The employee checked the sensors to see if its properly sending the data to Kinesis and verified that the data is indeed sent every day.

What could be the reason for this?

- Your AWS account was hacked and someone has deleted some data in your Kinesis stream.
- `By default, the data records are only accessible for 24 hours from the time they are added to a Kinesis stream.`
- An AWS Batch jobs consumer processed the data, but it missed records because batch jobs are not designed for streaming sources.
- There is a problem in the sensors. They probably had some intermittent connection hence, the data is not sent to the stream.



This is another **very important Kinesis exam pattern**. The key is to understand the difference between **Kinesis retention** and **SQS retention**.

## First, understand the scenario

The sensors send data:

```text
Sensors
   ↓
Kinesis Data Stream
   ↓
Consumer
   ↓
S3
```

The sensors send data **every day**.

But the consumer only runs **every other day**:

```text
Day 1 → data arrives
Day 2 → consumer processes
Day 3 → data arrives
Day 4 → consumer processes
```

The problem is that some data is missing from S3.

Why?

Because Kinesis doesn't keep data forever.

---

# The key concept: Kinesis retention period

When a record enters a **Kinesis Data Stream**, it is retained for a certain amount of time.

### Default:

**24 hours**

So imagine:

```text
Day 1, 10 AM
    ↓
Record enters Kinesis
    ↓
24 hours pass
    ↓
Day 2, 10 AM
    ↓
Record expires
```

If your consumer doesn't process that record before it expires, it is no longer available.

That's exactly what's happening here.

The consumer waits **every other day (~48 hours)**, but the default retention is only **24 hours**.

```text
Kinesis retention = 24 hours
Consumer interval = 48 hours

24 < 48
     ↓
Record can expire
     ↓
Consumer can't read it
     ↓
S3 doesn't receive it
```

---

# ✅ Correct answer

> **By default, the data records are only accessible for 24 hours from the time they are added to a Kinesis stream.**

---

# Why the other answers are wrong

### ❌ "AWS account was hacked"

Nothing in the scenario indicates a security problem.

The sensors were verified to be working.

More importantly, there is a very obvious Kinesis configuration issue.

---

### ❌ "Problem with the sensors"

The question explicitly tells us:

> The employee verified that the data is indeed being sent every day.

So the sensors are working.

---

### ❌ "AWS Batch consumer missed records"

This is a distraction.

The real issue is simply:

> **Consumer is too slow compared with Kinesis retention.**

---

# 🧠 The exam pattern you should memorize

For **Kinesis Data Streams**, remember:

> **Default retention = 24 hours**

Maximum:

> **365 days**

So:

```text
Kinesis Data Streams
        ↓
Default retention
        ↓
24 hours
        ↓
Can be increased
        ↓
Up to 365 days
```

---

# Very important: Kinesis vs SQS

Since you've already studied SQS, don't mix these up.

| Service                  | Default retention |      Maximum |
| ------------------------ | ----------------: | -----------: |
| **Kinesis Data Streams** |      **24 hours** | **365 days** |
| **SQS**                  |        **4 days** |  **14 days** |

This is a **great exam trap**.

### SQS

Think:

> **4 days → 14 days**

### Kinesis

Think:

> **24 hours → 365 days**

---

# What should the company do?

There are two possible approaches:

### Option 1 — Process more frequently

Instead of:

```text
Consumer → every 48 hours
```

make it process:

```text
Consumer → every 24 hours or more frequently
```

### Option 2 — Increase Kinesis retention

For example:

```text
Retention
24 hours
   ↓
48 hours+
```

Then the consumer has enough time to process the records.

---

# 🎯 SAA shortcut

Whenever you see:

> **Kinesis + consumer processes data later + missing records**

Immediately ask:

### "Is the record still inside the retention period?"

If default settings are mentioned:

> **Kinesis = 24 hours**

And if the consumer processes **after 24 hours**, records may already be gone.

### So for this question:

**Consumer every other day = ~48 hours**

**Kinesis default retention = 24 hours**

**48 > 24 → records expire → missing data**

✅ **Correct: Kinesis default retention is 24 hours.**
