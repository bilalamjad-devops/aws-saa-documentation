30. Question

Category: CSAA – Design Resilient Architectures

An online stock trading application stores financial data in an Amazon S3 bucket, with a lifecycle policy that moves older data to Glacier every month. A strict compliance requirement mandates that a surprise audit can occur at any time, and the required data must be retrievable in under 15 minutes under all circumstances. The manager has instructed that retrieval capacity be available when needed and should support up to 150 MB/s of retrieval throughput.

Which of the following will meet the given requirement? (Select TWO.)

- `Use Expedited Retrieval to access the financial data.`
- Specify a range, or portion, of the financial data archive to retrieve.
- `Purchase provisioned retrieval capacity.`
- Use Standard Retrieval for accessing the financial data.
- Use Bulk Retrieval to access the financial data.


## Question 30 — Glacier Retrieval

This one is testing **retrieval speed + guaranteed retrieval capacity**.

The requirement gives us two very important clues:

> **Data must be retrieved in under 15 minutes**
> **Retrieval throughput up to 150 MB/s must be available when needed**

So let's match the requirements.

### ✅ Correct answers

1. **Use Expedited Retrieval**
2. **Purchase Provisioned Retrieval Capacity**

---

### 1. Expedited Retrieval = Fast 🚀

S3 Glacier has different retrieval speeds.

For this question, remember:

| Retrieval     |    Approx. time | Use                          |
| ------------- | --------------: | ---------------------------- |
| **Expedited** | **1–5 minutes** | Urgent access                |
| **Standard**  |       3–5 hours | Normal retrieval             |
| **Bulk**      |      5–12 hours | Large, inexpensive retrieval |

The company needs:

> **< 15 minutes**

Therefore:

🚨 **Expedited Retrieval**

---

### 2. Why Provisioned Retrieval Capacity?

Here's the tricky part.

Normally, Expedited Retrieval is fast, **but AWS cannot guarantee that capacity will always be available during unusually high demand**.

The question says:

> **"under all circumstances"**

and

> **"retrieval capacity to be available when needed"**

That's the clue for:

🚨 **Provisioned Retrieval Capacity**

Provisioned capacity guarantees capacity for expedited retrievals.

Each provisioned capacity unit provides:

> **up to 150 MB/s retrieval throughput**

Which exactly matches the requirement.

---

### 🧠 Think of it like this

Imagine an emergency hospital.

**Expedited Retrieval** = emergency lane 🚑

It's much faster than the normal lane.

But during an extreme rush, the emergency lane might become crowded.

**Provisioned Retrieval Capacity** = you have **reserved capacity guaranteed for your emergency vehicles**.

So:

```text
S3 Glacier
    │
    ↓
Expedited Retrieval
    │
    ↓
1–5 minutes
    │
    +
    ↓
Provisioned Retrieval Capacity
    │
    ↓
Guaranteed capacity
up to 150 MB/s
```

---

## Why the other answers fail

### ❌ Standard Retrieval

**3–5 hours**

Requirement = **under 15 minutes**

So immediately eliminate it.

### ❌ Bulk Retrieval

**5–12 hours**

Again, nowhere near 15 minutes.

### ❌ Specify a range

Range retrieval lets you retrieve **only part of an archive**.

It doesn't guarantee the **retrieval capacity** the question specifically asks for.

---

# 🎯 SAA exam shortcut

When you see:

> **"Glacier + urgent retrieval in minutes"**

Think:

### **Expedited**

When you additionally see:

> **"guaranteed / available under all circumstances / predictable capacity"**

Think:

### **Provisioned Retrieval Capacity**

So the mental formula is:

> **Minutes → Expedited**
> **Guaranteed Expedited capacity → Provisioned Capacity**

This is a very good SAA pattern to memorize.
