12. Question

Category: CSAA – Design Cost-Optimized Architectures

A web application requires a minimum of six Amazon Elastic Compute Cloud (EC2) instances running at all times. You are tasked to deploy the application to three availability zones in the EU Ireland region (eu-west-1a, eu-west-1b, and eu-west-1c). It is required that the system is fault-tolerant up to the loss of one Availability Zone.

Which of the following setup is the most cost-effective solution which also maintains the fault-tolerance of your system?

- `3 instances in eu-west-1a, 3 instances in eu-west-1b, and 3 instances in eu-west-1c`
- 6 instances in eu-west-1a, 6 instances in eu-west-1b, and no instances in eu-west-1c
- 6 instances in eu-west-1a, 6 instances in eu-west-1b, and 6 instances in eu-west-1c
- 2 instances in eu-west-1a, 2 instances in eu-west-1b, and 2 instances in eu-west-1c

Yes. This question is testing a very useful **fault-tolerance calculation**.

## What is the question asking?

You need:

> **At least 6 EC2 instances running at all times.**

And they must be spread across:

* AZ-A
* AZ-B
* AZ-C

The system must survive:

> **Loss of any ONE Availability Zone.**

And among the valid solutions, choose the **cheapest**.

---

## The key calculation

If one AZ disappears, you still need **6 instances**.

Therefore, the two remaining AZs together must contain at least 6 instances.

The cheapest balanced setup is:

```text
AZ-A → 3
AZ-B → 3
AZ-C → 3
```

Total:

**9 instances**

Now imagine AZ-A completely fails:

```text
AZ-A ❌ → 0

AZ-B → 3
AZ-C → 3

Remaining = 6 ✅
```

If AZ-B fails:

```text
AZ-A → 3
AZ-B ❌ → 0
AZ-C → 3

Remaining = 6 ✅
```

If AZ-C fails:

```text
AZ-A → 3
AZ-B → 3
AZ-C ❌ → 0

Remaining = 6 ✅
```

So **3 + 3 + 3** works.

---

# Why not 2 + 2 + 2?

Initially:

```text
2 + 2 + 2 = 6
```

Looks good.

But lose one AZ:

```text
2 + 2 = 4 ❌
```

You need 6.

Therefore, it isn't fault-tolerant.

---

# Why not 6 + 6 + 6?

It definitely works:

```text
6 + 6 + 6 = 18
```

Lose one AZ:

```text
6 + 6 = 12 ✅
```

But you're paying for **18 instances** when only 9 are necessary.

The question says:

> **MOST COST-EFFECTIVE**

So 18 is wasteful.

---

# Why not 6 + 6 + 0?

You have:

```text
AZ-A → 6
AZ-B → 6
AZ-C → 0
```

Lose AZ-A:

```text
AZ-B → 6
AZ-C → 0

6 ✅
```

Lose AZ-B:

```text
AZ-A → 6
AZ-C → 0

6 ✅
```

But there is an important issue: **the system isn't actually deployed across all three AZs**, despite the requirement saying to deploy it to the three AZs.

And it uses **12 instances**, while 9 is cheaper.

---

# 🧠 The general formula

This is the useful exam trick.

If:

* Required minimum = **N**
* Must tolerate loss of **1 AZ**
* Number of AZs = **3**

You need enough instances so that after losing the largest AZ, **N remain**.

For equal distribution:

> **N ÷ (number of AZs − 1)**

Here:

```text
6 ÷ (3 - 1)
= 6 ÷ 2
= 3
```

Therefore:

```text
3 per AZ
```

Total:

```text
3 × 3 = 9
```

### ⭐ Memorize:

> **To survive losing 1 of 3 AZs while maintaining 6 instances, run 3 in each AZ → 9 total.**

And notice the distinction:

**High availability** → "Can I still operate after failure?"

**Fault tolerance** → "Can I continue meeting the required capacity/performance after failure?"

That's why SAA questions often give you a **minimum capacity requirement**.
