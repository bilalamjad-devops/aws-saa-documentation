10. Question

Category: CSAA – Design Cost-Optimized Architectures

A media company stores approximately 250 TB of archived video and audio assets on portable storage systems in an on-premises facility. The company needs to import the archive into Amazon S3 for long-term storage, durability, and redundancy. The company currently has a 100 Mbps dedicated line connecting its head office to the Internet. The team considered using AWS Storage Gateway Volume Gateway, but determined that the 100 Mbps connection would take weeks to transfer the full dataset.

Which of the following is the FASTEST and MOST COST-EFFECTIVE way to import all this data to S3?

- Use S3 Transfer Acceleration to speed up the upload.
- `Import the archived dataset to S3 through AWS Data Transfer Terminal.`
- Upload it directly to S3
- Establish a direct connection using AWS Direct Connect, then transfer the data over to S3.

Exactly. This question is testing **how to move a HUGE amount of data into AWS when your internet connection is too slow**.

Let's break it down simply.

## 1. What is the question saying?

Company has:

* **250 TB** of old video/audio files
* Files are currently **on-premises**
* They want to put them into **Amazon S3**
* Their internet connection is only **100 Mbps**
* Uploading 250 TB over 100 Mbps would take a very long time
* They want the **fastest + most cost-effective** solution

So the real question is:

> **"I have a huge amount of data physically sitting with me. My internet is too slow. How can I get it into S3 quickly?"**

Answer:

✅ **AWS Data Transfer Terminal**

---

# 2. What does "archived dataset" mean?

**Archive = old data that you don't frequently use but need to keep.**

For example, a media company might have:

```text
2020 Videos
2021 Videos
2022 Videos
Old TV recordings
Old customer videos
Old audio files
```

They don't need to access these files every day, but they need to **preserve them for years**.

That's an **archived dataset**.

In this question:

> 250 TB of archived video/audio

means:

**250 TB of old files sitting in their on-premises storage that they want to move to S3 for long-term storage.**

---

# 3. What is AWS Data Transfer Terminal?

This is the important new concept.

Imagine you have:

**250 TB of data sitting on physical storage.**

Instead of doing:

```text
Your storage
     ↓
100 Mbps Internet
     ↓
AWS
     ↓
S3
```

which could take a very long time...

You physically take your storage devices to an **AWS Data Transfer Terminal**.

Conceptually:

```text
Your physical storage
        ↓
AWS Data Transfer Terminal
        ↓
High-speed AWS network
        ↓
Amazon S3
```

So you're essentially saying:

> **"My internet is too slow, so I'll physically bring my data to an AWS facility and transfer it using AWS's high-speed network."**

That's why it is particularly useful for **very large one-time data transfers**.

---

# 4. Why not Direct Upload?

The company has only:

**100 Mbps**

That's the bottleneck.

Even though S3 can accept enormous amounts of data, **your upload speed is limited by your connection**.

Think:

```text
S3 = huge highway
Your office Internet = tiny road
```

Making S3 bigger doesn't make your 100 Mbps connection faster.

❌ Direct upload → too slow.

---

# 5. Why not S3 Transfer Acceleration?

S3 Transfer Acceleration is useful when you have users geographically far from an S3 bucket and want to improve upload performance using AWS edge locations.

Conceptually:

```text
You
 ↓
AWS Edge Location
 ↓
AWS network
 ↓
S3
```

But you're **still starting with your 100 Mbps Internet connection**.

So:

**250 TB + 100 Mbps = still a huge transfer.**

❌ Not the best answer here.

---

# 6. Why not Direct Connect?

Direct Connect gives you a **dedicated network connection between your on-premises environment and AWS**.

It's excellent when you need:

> **continuous, predictable connectivity between on-premises and AWS.**

For example:

```text
Company Data Center
        ↕
   Direct Connect
        ↕
       AWS
```

But this question is basically:

> "I have 250 TB that I need to move once."

Setting up dedicated connectivity for a one-time migration can be unnecessary.

❌ Direct Connect → better for **ongoing hybrid connectivity**, not this one-time bulk import.

---

When you see:

### **Huge amount of data + physical data + slow Internet**

Think:

> 🚨 **Physical data transfer solution**

In this question:

**AWS Data Transfer Terminal** ✅

Exactly — **you do NOT need an AWS Region physically inside Pakistan** to use AWS.

For example, suppose you're in Pakistan and your S3 bucket is in **Mumbai (`ap-south-1`)** or another AWS Region. You can transfer data from Pakistan to that S3 bucket over the Internet. AWS's current Region list includes Mumbai, UAE, Bahrain, etc., but no Pakistan Region. ([AWS Documentation][1])

### What about Data Transfer Terminal?

This is where an important distinction comes in.

The **Data Transfer Terminal is not necessarily in the same country as your AWS Region**. AWS describes it as a physical, network-ready location where you bring your storage device and transfer data to AWS. AWS says the terminal can connect to **any AWS Region accessible from the Internet**. ([AWS Documentation][2])

So conceptually:

```text
Pakistan
   │
   │  250 TB on your HDD/SSD
   ▼
Travel to an AWS Data Transfer Terminal
   │
   │  High-speed AWS network
   ▼
Amazon S3
(e.g. Mumbai / UAE / another Region)
```

You **don't travel to an "AWS Region in Pakistan"**. You travel to an available Data Transfer Terminal facility.

### But here's the exam takeaway

Don't overthink the physical geography in the SAA question.

The question gives you:

> **250 TB + only 100 Mbps Internet + one-time migration**

AWS wants you to recognize:

**Huge amount of data + slow network → physical data transfer solution.**

For the current AWS service, that's **Data Transfer Terminal**. AWS says it is intended for bringing your own storage devices to a physical facility for fast transfer. ([AWS Documentation][2])

Also, one current detail: **Data Transfer Terminal is currently available only to AWS Enterprise Support customers**. ([AWS Documentation][2])

| Internet upload but optimize long-distance transfer | 🟠 **S3 Transfer Acceleration** |

So your mental model should be:

**"No AWS Region in my country doesn't matter. My S3 bucket lives in an AWS Region somewhere else, and I transfer data to that Region."**
