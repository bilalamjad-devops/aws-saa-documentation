33. Question

Category: CSAA – Design High-Performing Architectures

A tech company has a CRM application hosted on an Auto Scaling group of On-Demand EC2 instances with different instance types and sizes. The application is extensively used during office hours from 9 in the morning to 5 in the afternoon. Their users are complaining that the performance of the application is slow during the start of the day but then works normally after a couple of hours.

Which of the following is the MOST operationally efficient solution to implement to ensure the application works properly at the beginning of the day?

- Configure a Dynamic scaling policy for the Auto Scaling group to launch new instances based on the CPU utilization.
- `Configure a Scheduled scaling policy for the Auto Scaling group to launch new instances before the start of the day.`
- Configure a Dynamic scaling policy for the Auto Scaling group to launch new instances based on the Memory utilization.
- Configure a Predictive scaling policy for the Auto Scaling group to automatically adjust the number of Amazon EC2 instances

Yes, this is another **easy but important ASG scaling question**.

The key is the phrase:

> **"Extensively used from 9 AM to 5 PM"**
> **"Slow at the start of the day"**

The traffic pattern is **predictable**.

### 3 scaling types you should know

| Scaling                | When to use                                         | Example                                             |
| ---------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| **Dynamic scaling**    | Traffic changes unpredictably                       | CPU goes above 70% → add EC2                        |
| **Scheduled scaling**  | Traffic changes at known times                      | 8:30 AM → add EC2                                   |
| **Predictive scaling** | AWS predicts future demand from historical patterns | AWS forecasts tomorrow's traffic → scale beforehand |

### Why Scheduled Scaling?

If you already know:

```text id="c9q7v4"
9 AM → traffic increases
5 PM → traffic decreases
```

Don't wait for CPU to become high.

Instead:

```text id="o3x8r2"
8:30 AM
   ↓
ASG launches instances
   ↓
9:00 AM
   ↓
Users arrive
   ↓
Instances are READY ✅
```

That's why **Scheduled Scaling** is the best answer.

### Why not Dynamic Scaling?

Dynamic scaling might work like:

```text id="b0n2vh"
9:00 AM
Users arrive
   ↓
CPU increases
   ↓
ASG detects high CPU
   ↓
Launch EC2
   ↓
Instance boots
```

During that period, users can experience the **slow performance** described in the question.

So:

> **Predictable time-based traffic → Scheduled Scaling.**

### Why not Predictive Scaling?

Predictive scaling is useful when AWS needs to **forecast future demand based on historical usage**.

But this question gives us a very simple, known schedule:

> **9 AM–5 PM**

So scheduled scaling is simpler and more operationally efficient.

Also, the question says the ASG has **different instance types and sizes**. The provided explanation highlights that predictive scaling works best with homogeneous capacity, whereas this ASG is heterogeneous.

### 🧠 Exam shortcut

Remember:

> **Known time → Scheduled**
> **Unknown/unpredictable demand → Dynamic**
> **Historical patterns + forecasting → Predictive**

For this question:

**9 AM predictable traffic → Scheduled Scaling.**
